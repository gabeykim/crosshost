# Cell-free lysates largely abolish the cross-host differences that dominate in-vivo measurements

*(Publication-ready results-section prose. All numbers sourced from `out/GATE10_MEMO.md` Tasks 4.1-4.3 and `out/results/gate10_*`; no new analysis was run to produce this section beyond what Gate 10 already established.)*

To test whether the *B. subtilis* cross-host discrepancy reflects transcriptional machinery or broader cellular context, we compared our in-vivo measurements against DRAFTS (Yim, Johns et al., 2019), an independent dataset from the same laboratory that characterized transcription from the same regulatory-sequence library using cell-free lysates across ten bacterial species. Cell-free transcription-translation (TXTL) systems retain core transcriptional machinery — RNA polymerase, sigma factors, ribonucleotides — but lack an intact membrane, native chromosomal supercoiling, macromolecular resource competition, and growth-phase-dependent physiology. If the chassis effect this project studies is substantially a property of transcriptional machinery, it should persist in a cell-free system built from that machinery. If it is not, cell-free measurements offer a natural test of what is missing.

**It does not persist.** Cross-host transcription correlations in DRAFTS span **0.623–0.911** across all 45 pairwise species comparisons, including comparisons that cross phylum boundaries (Proteobacteria vs. Firmicutes vs. Actinobacteria). This is a strikingly narrow, uniformly high band. For the one pair directly comparable between the two studies — *E. coli* and *B. subtilis*, the only two of our three primary hosts present in DRAFTS ( *P. aeruginosa* was not tested; see Limitations) — the cell-free cross-host correlation is **0.597**, more than double the in-vivo figure of **0.258** computed on the identical 165 bp sequence library. *B. subtilis* itself, a severe outlier in vivo, is unremarkable in cell-free: its mean correlation with the other nine DRAFTS species (0.783) sits near the middle of the distribution, indistinguishable from several Proteobacteria.

This is not a general failure of the cell-free system to reproduce a given host's own biology. DRAFTS separately measured 234 sequences in both cell-free and in-vivo formats for seven species, allowing a direct same-host, cross-modality comparison. **Within-species agreement is good**: Spearman correlations between the cell-free and in-vivo measurement of the same species range from **0.69 to 0.90** across all seven species, including *B. subtilis* (0.69). *(This per-species figure was not published as a clean extractable table — the paper's own comparison appears only as annotations on scatter-plot figures — and was instead recomputed directly from DRAFTS's own released source data for this exact comparison; it should closely track, but is not a literal quote of, the paper's reported values.)* Cell-free measurement is therefore a reasonably faithful proxy for a given host's own transcriptional behavior. What it does not reproduce is the *difference between hosts* — the specific quantity a chassis-effect study needs.

## Mechanistic reading

*(Interpretation, consistent with the data above but not a demonstrated causal claim — flagged explicitly as such.)*

Cell-free lysates retain the enzymatic core of transcription and remove the membrane, native chromosomal architecture, growth-phase physiology, and resource-competition context a living cell provides. If host-specific transcriptional differences largely disappear when that context is removed, host specificity plausibly resides substantially in cellular context — how a cell's physiological state modulates the shared transcriptional machinery — rather than in fixed differences in the machinery itself. This reading is consistent with, and offers a candidate mechanism for, this project's own central negative result: every genomic host-feature vector this project built (sigma-factor complement, anti-Shine-Dalgarno energetics, codon usage, tRNA copy number) describes transcriptional and translational *machinery*. If host specificity lives predominantly in physiological *context* rather than machinery composition, a feature vector built entirely from machinery annotations would be expected to carry little signal regardless of how well-constructed it is — which is consistent with, though does not by itself prove, why 37 such genomic features were statistically indistinguishable from an arbitrary host identity tag (H-DIAGNOSTIC, `out/PAPER_FRAMING.md` finding 5). **We state this as a hypothesis the data is consistent with, not a demonstrated mechanism** — confirming it would require an experiment this project cannot run (e.g., titrating physiological context back into a cell-free system, or measuring the same library in vivo across a matched growth-condition series in multiple hosts).

## Implications for cell-free prototyping

Cell-free expression systems are an active area of synthetic-biology tooling investment, in part because they promise faster design-build-test cycles than transforming and growing a living host. Our comparison suggests a specific, actionable boundary on that promise: **cell-free measurement appears reliable for predicting how a regulatory part will behave within a given host, but unreliable for predicting how that behavior will differ across hosts.** A practitioner screening parts for use in a single target chassis via a matched cell-free lysate should expect results broadly consistent with in-vivo behavior (within-species agreement 0.69–0.90). A practitioner trying to anticipate whether a part characterized in one host's cell-free system will transfer to a different host in vivo should not expect the cell-free cross-host comparison to be informative — the two hosts may look far more similar cell-free than they will behave in living cells.

## Limitations

- ***P. aeruginosa*, one of this project's three primary hosts, is entirely absent from DRAFTS.** DRAFTS's ten-species panel includes *Pantoea agglomerans* (also abbreviated "Pa" in the source tables — a genuine naming collision that could mislead careless reuse of both datasets together, see `out/KNOWN_ISSUES.md`), not *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*. The modality bridge above therefore rests on two shared primary hosts (*E. coli*, *B. subtilis*) plus indirect support from the RS234 seven-species in-vivo/in-vitro design, which does not include *P. aeruginosa* either.
- **The sequence-level join between the two datasets is small.** Despite both libraries being built from the same underlying 184-genome mining effort by the same laboratory, their oligo ID numbering schemes are disjoint (a batch artifact, not a data problem — see `out/GATE10_MEMO.md` Task 3), and exact-sequence-text matching recovers only 112 of DRAFTS's 1,047 sequences (10.7%) as also present in our three-host library. The headline EC–BS comparison (0.597 vs. 0.258) uses DRAFTS's full library correlation and our full-library in-vivo correlation separately, not a paired within-sequence comparison at scale — a same-sequence paired comparison was attempted but the overlap (n=15 sequences with usable in-vivo data on both hosts) was too small to be informative and is not reported as a standalone figure.
- **DRAFTS measures transcription only.** No cell-free translation comparison is possible; this section's conclusions are scoped to transcriptional host-specificity and say nothing about whether the same pattern would hold for translation.
- **n=10 hosts in the cell-free modality, n=3 in the in-vivo modality this project's central claim is built on** (n=7 for the RS234 in-vivo/in-vitro design specifically). The breadth of the cell-free panel does not transfer to the in-vivo comparison this section's central claim depends on.

## Why a host-count sweep was not run on DRAFTS

DRAFTS's ten-host panel was evaluated for a possible follow-up: training a host-conditioned model across varying numbers of DRAFTS hosts to test whether conditioning failure in this project's main results was an artifact of having only two training hosts per fold. That sweep was not run. The finding above is the reason: cell-free cross-host correlations are uniformly high and *B. subtilis* is not an outlier in that modality, so a sweep conducted there would be well-powered to answer a real question about cell-free systems specifically, but could not resolve whether more training hosts would fix conditioning on the in-vivo problem this project's central claim is actually about — the two modalities are shown above to measure substantially different phenomena. Full reasoning: `out/GATE10_MEMO.md` Task 5.
