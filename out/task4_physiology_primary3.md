# Primary-Source Verification: Published Physiology Metrics for E. coli, B. subtilis, P. aeruginosa

**Scope:** Verify whether reusable, quantitative growth-law / resource-allocation physiology data exist in the primary literature for *Escherichia coli*, *Bacillus subtilis*, and *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*, and whether the Bernstein lab's own chassis-effect papers publish such a table for these three hosts specifically.

**Evidence tags:** [VERIFIED] = source was read directly, citation given. [INFERRED] = reasoning beyond what the source states, confidence flagged. [NOT FOUND] = searched, nothing located; queries/sources tried are listed.

---

## 0. Bottom line on the Bernstein lab papers (read first — this changes how to read the table below)

**The Bernstein lab's chassis-effect papers do NOT test the three-host set (E. coli + B. subtilis + P. aeruginosa) that this benchmark needs.** [VERIFIED]

Papers identified and read (via WebFetch of PMC/bioRxiv full text or PDF):

1. **Chan DTC, Baldwin GS, Bernstein HC. "Revealing the Host-Dependent Nature of an Engineered Genetic Inverter in Concordance with Physiology." *BioDesign Research* 2023 (also bioRxiv 2023.02.27.529268; PMC10432152).**
   Hosts tested: *E. coli*, *Halopseudomonas aestusnigri* VGXO14, *Halopseudomonas oceani* KX20, *Pseudomonas deceptionensis* M1, *Pseudomonas fluorescens* SBW25, *Pseudomonas putida* — six Gammaproteobacteria, all cultured in **LB at 30°C**.
   Physiology table: **Figure 3G / Figure 1C** report maximum specific growth rate (μ, h⁻¹), specific carrying capacity (OD600), plasmid copy number, genomic GC content, and codon adaptation index (CAI) per host — but **no ribosome content, RNA polymerase abundance, RNA/protein ratio, or proteome-sector data**. Approximate growth rates read from Fig. 1C: *P. putida* 0.67 ± 0.01 h⁻¹, *P. fluorescens* ~0.50 h⁻¹, *P. deceptionensis* ~0.48 h⁻¹, *E. coli* ~0.50–0.55 h⁻¹, *H. oceani*/*H. aestusnigri* ~0.22–0.25 h⁻¹ (exact numeric values for the latter three are shown only graphically in the source, not stated in text).
   **B. subtilis and P. aeruginosa are absent from this study.**

2. **Chan DTC, et al. "Pangenomic landscapes shape performances of a synthetic genetic circuit across Stutzerimonas species." *mSystems* 2024 (bioRxiv 2024.02.15.580380; PMC11406997).**
   Hosts: six *Stutzerimonas* species (*S. chloritidismutans*, *S. perfectomarina*, *S. degradans*, *S. pgs16*, *S. pgs17*, *S. stutzeri*), with *E. coli* DH5α used only as a cloning/reference host, not a primary experimental chassis. Growth parameters (max specific growth rate μ, carrying capacity A, lag time λ) are reported in **Figure 3** per *Stutzerimonas* strain, but again no ribosome/RNAP/RNA-protein-ratio/proteome-sector data, and **no B. subtilis or P. aeruginosa**.

3. Related Bernstein-lab work identified by search but not needed further: "A broad-host-range event detector: expanding and quantifying performance between Escherichia coli and Pseudomonas species" (*Synthetic Biology*, Oxford Academic) — again *E. coli* vs. *P. putida*/*P. fluorescens*, not *P. aeruginosa* or *B. subtilis*.

**Conclusion on Bernstein lab:** Across every chassis-effect paper located, their host panel is *E. coli* plus a set of **environmental, non-pathogenic Pseudomonads/Halopseudomonads/Stutzerimonas** — never *B. subtilis*, never *P. aeruginosa*. They do publish physiology tables/figures (growth rate, carrying capacity, lag time, GC content, plasmid copy number, CAI), but the specific host set for those tables never overlaps with this benchmark's three target organisms simultaneously. **No Bernstein-lab table can be reused as-is for an E. coli / B. subtilis / P. aeruginosa comparison.**

## 2. Detail notes per host

### 2.1 *Escherichia coli* — by far the best-characterized of the three
- Growth-law parameters (RNA/protein ratio vs. growth rate, proteome-sector fractions, translational/nutritional capacities) were literally *defined* on E. coli data: Scott et al. 2010 *Science* is the origin paper for this entire framework, and Bremer & Dennis's classic tabulation (ribosomes and RNAP per cell across doubling times 24–100 min, 37°C, strain B/r) underlies essentially all downstream cross-species comparisons, including the P. aeruginosa and B. subtilis papers found below (both cite Bremer & Dennis as the basis for using RNA/protein ratio as a ribosome-content proxy).
- Caveat: even within "E. coli," reported numbers vary by strain (B/r vs. K-12 derivatives), by exact medium composition, and by measurement method (bulk biochemistry vs. single-cell microscopy) — e.g., M9+glucose doubling time is reported as 38 min by single-cell tracking (Reshes et al. 2008) vs. the more commonly repeated ~60 min bulk estimate. These are not contradictory so much as method/strain-dependent; anyone reusing a number needs to also pin the strain, not just medium+temperature.

### 2.2 *Bacillus subtilis* — good growth-rate/RNA-protein-ratio data, weak ribosome/RNAP absolute-count data
- The standout finding is **Zhu, Mori, Hwa & Dai, *PNAS* 2025 (122:e2427091122)**, "Distantly related bacteria share a rigid proteome allocation strategy with flexible enzyme kinetics." This paper grows *E. coli* and *B. subtilis* (plus *Vibrio natriegens*) side-by-side, in the *same* media (including LB) at the *same* temperatures (37°C primary, plus 30°C and 43°C), and directly reports RNA/protein ratio and proteome-sector allocation for both. This is the single best directly-comparable, same-paper physiology dataset found for any two of this benchmark's three hosts. It does **not** include P. aeruginosa.
- Its central finding is itself notable for the benchmark's framing question: E. coli and B. subtilis (two very distantly related model bacteria) were found to have a **nearly identical maximal transcription+translation proteome allocation (~40% in LB)** and near-identical RNA/protein ratio in LB — i.e., under matched growth conditions, "phylogenetically distant" hosts converge on similar physiology, which is directly in the spirit of the Bernstein lab's own claim, just demonstrated with different hosts and a different (proteomics/growth-law) methodology.
- Absolute ribosome and RNA-polymerase molecule counts for B. subtilis are comparatively thin: only a single non-growth-rate-resolved ribosome count (~6,000/cell, Barrera & Pan 2004) was located, and no RNAP molecules/cell number was found at all.

### 2.3 *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* — growth rate is well documented; deeper resource-allocation numbers are largely absent
- A solid, frequently-cited reference growth rate exists: **24–27 min generation time in LB at 37°C for lab strains PAO1/PA14** (Yang et al. 2008, *J Bacteriol*), giving μ ≈ 1.5–1.7 h⁻¹ — broadly comparable in magnitude to E. coli's LB growth rate (μ ≈ 1.4–2.0 h⁻¹, Sezonov et al. 2007) and P. aeruginosa's own faster-than-B.-subtilis-minimal-medium range.
- Beyond growth rate, however, the trail goes cold: no absolute ribosome count, no RNA-polymerase molecule count, and no classic bulk RNA/protein-ratio-vs.-growth-rate dataset (in the Scott/Hwa sense) could be located for P. aeruginosa in the literature searched. The one growth-rate-resolved proteomics/transcriptomics dataset that exists (Zhang et al. 2023, *mBio*, PMC9973009) is explicitly designed around slow, chemostat-imposed doubling times (3.3–25.3 h) meant to model chronic cystic-fibrosis lung infection — these growth rates are 10–100× slower than the LB/minimal-medium batch conditions used in the E. coli and B. subtilis growth-law studies above, so even where that paper does touch on RNA/protein-type relationships, the growth-rate range is not overlapping or comparable.

## 3. Comparability assessment across the three hosts

**Growth rate/doubling time (metric 1) is the only one of the six metrics with plausibly comparable, medium/temperature-matched data across all three hosts**, and even then only approximately:
- All three have LB, 37°C reference points: E. coli μ ≈ 1.4–2.0 h⁻¹ (Sezonov 2007); B. subtilis grown alongside E. coli in LB at 37°C in Zhu et al. 2025 (exact digit not extracted, but paper implies rates close to E. coli's); P. aeruginosa μ ≈ 1.5–1.7 h⁻¹ (Yang 2008, LB 37°C). These three numbers are at least nominally condition-matched (same medium name, same temperature), though strain differences (B/r vs. K-12 vs. PAO1/PA14 vs. B. subtilis 168-derivatives) and measurement-method differences (bulk OD vs. single-cell) are not controlled for across papers.
- M9/minimal-medium data exist for E. coli (~38–60 min doubling) and B. subtilis (Borkowski's 8 minimal media, 0.25–1.70 h⁻¹, 37°C), but the specific minimal-medium recipes differ (E. coli M9+glucose vs. B. subtilis's M9-variant panel), so these are not strictly the same medium even though both are "M9-based, 37°C." No matched M9+glucose, 37°C data point for P. aeruginosa was found.

**RNA/protein ratio and proteome allocation to translation (metrics 4–5) are comparable for E. coli vs. B. subtilis only**, via the single Zhu et al. 2025 PNAS paper that measured both under literally identical media and temperatures in the same study. **P. aeruginosa has no equivalent dataset**, so a three-way comparison on these metrics is not currently possible from the primary literature located.

**Ribosome content and RNA polymerase abundance (metrics 2–3) are well resolved only for E. coli** (Bremer & Dennis's classic growth-rate series, 37°C). For B. subtilis only a single, condition-unspecified ribosome count exists and no RNAP count was found. For P. aeruginosa, neither metric was found at all. **A three-way comparison on these two metrics is not runnable from published data as things stand.**

**Overall:** of the six requested metrics, only growth rate/doubling time has data for all three hosts (with real but manageable medium/strain heterogeneity); RNA/protein ratio and translation-proteome-allocation are matched for two of three hosts (E. coli, B. subtilis) via one strong 2025 paper; absolute ribosome counts and RNA polymerase abundance are essentially E. coli-only. **The Bernstein lab's own papers do not supply any of this for the E. coli/B. subtilis/P. aeruginosa combination** — their physiology tables exist, but for a different host panel (E. coli plus various environmental Pseudomonads/Halopseudomonads/Stutzerimonas), never including B. subtilis or P. aeruginosa.
